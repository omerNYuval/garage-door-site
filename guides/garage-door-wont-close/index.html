Why won’t my garage door close?

A garage door that won’t close leaves your home open. The good news: most of the time it’s something small you can check yourself in a few minutes.

Short answer

If the door starts closing and then reverses, or the opener light blinks, the cause is usually the safety sensors near the floor. Clean the lenses, clear anything in the way, and check that both sensor lights are on and steady.

Why the safety sensors matter so much

Since 1993, federal safety rules have required garage door openers sold in the US to reverse automatically when something is in the door’s path. The two small sensors near the floor are how the opener knows. If they can’t see each other, the opener assumes something is blocking the door and refuses to close it. That’s why sensors are the cause of most “won’t close” calls.

Check these first

  • Look at the safety sensors. The two small eyes near the bottom of the tracks must face each other. Wipe the lenses with a soft cloth, remove anything in the path, and check that both indicator lights are steady. A blinking or dark light usually means they’re out of line or a wire is loose.
  • Realign a sensor that’s been bumped. Sensors are easy to knock with a bike, a bin or a car bumper. Loosen the wing nut, point it straight at the other sensor until its light goes solid, then tighten it again.
  • Check the lock button. Many wall controls have a lock or vacation button that disables remotes. If the remote does nothing but the wall button works, this is often why.
  • Replace the remote batteries. If the wall button works and the remote doesn’t, try new batteries before anything else.
  • Look at the tracks and rollers. A bent track, a loose roller or debris in the track can stop the door partway down.
  • Check the power. Make sure the opener is plugged in and the outlet works. If the opener is dead, check the breaker or GFCI outlet.

Stop and call a technician if the door is crooked, a cable is loose or hanging, you heard a loud bang from above the door, or the door feels very heavy. These point to a broken spring or cable, and forcing the door can make it fall.

Common causes in Indianapolis

Two local patterns show up again and again. In winter, ice and packed snow at the threshold can freeze the bottom seal to the floor or block the sensors, so the door reverses or won’t seal. In late afternoon, low sun shining straight down a west-facing driveway can blind a sensor, so the door works in the morning and fails at dinnertime. Clearing ice, adding a small sun shield or swapping the sensors’ sides usually fixes both.

Common questions

Can I close the door manually?

If the springs and cables look intact, you can pull the red emergency release cord and close the door by hand. If the door is crooked or very heavy, don’t. Leave it and call for help.

Can I just bypass the sensors?

No. The sensors are a required safety feature that stops the door from closing on a child, pet or car. Fix the alignment instead of disabling them.

Is a door that won’t close an emergency?

It can be, because an open door leaves your home unsecured. If you can’t close it safely, call and ask for an urgent visit.
