How long do garage door springs last?

Springs are the part of your garage door that wears out on a schedule. Knowing roughly when yours are due helps you replace them before they snap on a cold Indianapolis morning.

Short answer

Most standard garage door springs are rated for about 10,000 cycles. One cycle is the door opening and closing once. For a household that uses the door 3 to 4 times a day, that works out to roughly 7 to 9 years.

What shortens a spring’s life

  • Heavy use. If the garage is your main entrance, the door may cycle 8 to 10 times a day, which can wear a standard spring out in 3 to 4 years.
  • Cold weather. Steel springs are under more stress in freezing temperatures. In central Indiana, a lot of springs give out on the first hard cold snaps of the winter.
  • Rust. Humidity and road salt carried in on cars corrode the coils, which adds friction and weakens the steel.
  • A heavier door. Adding insulation, a new panel or decorative hardware without adjusting the springs makes them work harder.
  • No maintenance. Springs that are never lubricated or checked wear faster.

Warning signs your springs are near the end

  • The door feels heavy. With the opener disconnected, a balanced door should stay put about halfway up. If it drops, the springs have lost tension.
  • You see gaps or stretched coils. A torsion spring should be a tight, even coil. A gap means it has broken. Uneven spacing on extension springs means they’re stretched.
  • The door is jerky or uneven. One side lifting faster than the other often means one spring is weaker than its partner.
  • The opener strains or slows down. When springs weaken, the opener ends up doing the lifting it was never built for.

How to make springs last longer

  • Lubricate the springs lightly with a garage-door lubricant two or three times a year. Avoid heavy grease, which collects dirt.
  • Do a balance test every few months, especially before winter.
  • Ask about high-cycle springs when it’s time to replace. They cost more up front, but can last two to three times as long.
  • Replace both springs together on a two-spring door, so they wear evenly.

Garage door springs in Indianapolis

Indianapolis winters swing between mild days and hard freezes, and that repeated stress is tough on steel springs. Many local homeowners first notice a problem on a cold morning when the door opens a few inches and stops. If your springs are more than 7 years old, a quick check in the fall can save you from being stuck in, or stuck out, in January.

Common questions

Do torsion and extension springs last the same amount of time?

Both are commonly rated around 10,000 cycles. Torsion springs are generally considered more durable and safer when they fail, which is why most newer doors use them.

Should I replace springs before they break?

If your springs are past their expected life and showing warning signs, replacing them on your schedule is easier than an emergency call when one snaps.

Can I tell how many cycles my springs have left?

Not exactly, but the age of the springs and how often you use the door give a good estimate. A technician can also check tension and wear during a tune-up.
