Safety · Broken spring

Is it safe to open a garage door with a broken spring?

A snapped spring often makes a loud bang, like a firecracker in the garage. Here’s what that means for your door and what to do next.

Short answer

No. The springs do the heavy lifting, not the opener. With a broken spring, the door can be too heavy to lift safely and can fall suddenly. Leave it closed and call a technician.

How much weight are we talking about?

~100–150 lb
a typical single steel door
200+ lb
many double or insulated doors
~10 lb
what a balanced door should feel like to lift

The springs counterbalance almost all of that weight. When one breaks, the full weight lands on the opener, the cables and whoever is lifting the door.

Why it’s dangerous

  • The door can drop. Without spring tension, a door that’s partly open can come down fast. Garage doors are a known cause of injuries in the US every year.
  • The opener can be damaged. Openers are built to guide a balanced door, not lift its full weight. Running it can strip gears or burn out the motor.
  • Cables can snap. The extra strain can break lift cables, which makes the door hang crooked or fall on one side.

Signs a spring is broken

  • A loud bang from the garage, often when no one was using the door.
  • A visible gap of a few inches in the coiled spring above the door.
  • The door opens only a few inches, then stops, or the opener hums and nothing moves.
  • The door feels extremely heavy when you try to lift it by hand.
  • Cables are loose or hanging, or the door sits crooked.

What to do right now

  • Don’t use the opener. Unplug it if you can, so no one presses the button by accident.
  • Leave the door where it is. If it’s closed, keep it closed. If it’s stuck open, keep people, pets and cars away from underneath it.
  • Secure your home. If the door is stuck open, lock the door from the garage into the house.
  • Don’t try to replace the spring yourself. Springs are under extreme tension. Releasing or winding one without the right tools can cause serious injury.
  • Call for a repair. A broken spring is a common repair, and most are done in a single visit.

Why springs often break in winter in Indianapolis

Cold temperatures add stress to steel springs that are already near the end of their life, so many Indianapolis homeowners hear that bang on the first freezing mornings of the season. If your car is trapped inside on a workday, say so when you call so the job can be prioritized.

Common questions

My car is stuck inside. What can I do?

Don’t force the door. Call and explain that your car is trapped, and ask for the earliest appointment.

Why did the spring break when no one was using the door?

Springs wear out after a certain number of open and close cycles. They often snap while the door is closed, when the spring is wound to full tension.

Should both springs be replaced?

If your door has two springs, replacing both at once is usually recommended, since the other one has the same wear.
